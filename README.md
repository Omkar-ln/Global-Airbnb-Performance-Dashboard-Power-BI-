Global Airbnb Performance Dashboard | Power BI

Project Overview
In this project, I built a Power BI dashboard to understand how Airbnb performs across 10 cities around the world. I looked at how many listings there are, how much they cost, how guests rate them, and how much people can trust the hosts.

The dashboard puts everything in one place, so anyone can click around and quickly see which cities, room types, and hosts stand out.

Business Objective
The goal was to answer simple questions like:

How has Airbnb grown over the years?
Which cities have the most listings and reviews?
Which room types are the most expensive?
Which cities do guests love the most (and the least)?
When do people travel the most?
Can guests trust the hosts?

I used KPIs and interactive visuals to find patterns and differences that a business team could act on.

Key Performance Indicators
The dashboard shows these main numbers at the top:

Listings – 2,79,712 places listed on Airbnb.
Cities – 10 cities covered.
Hosts – 1,82,024 hosts.
Property Types – 144 different types of properties.
Reviews – 5,373K guest reviews.

Analysis Performed
1. New Listings Over Time
I tracked how many new listings were added each year from 2008 to 2020, split by room type (entire place, private room, shared room, hotel room).

I also divided Airbnb's story into six phases: Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Decline, Reinvention, and COVID-19. New listings peaked in 2015, slowed down in 2016–2017 because of stricter local rules, grew again from 2018, and then dropped sharply in 2020 due to COVID-19.

2. Market Share by City
I compared the number of listings in each city and added a cumulative % line to show how much the top cities contribute.

Paris, New York, and Sydney together make up almost half of all listings and 59% of all reviews. Paris is at the top. The chart also shows how many listings belong to Superhosts.

3. Average Price by Room Type
I compared average prices across room types. Hotel rooms are the most expensive ($800), then entire places ($673), shared rooms ($580), and private rooms ($462).

4. Ratings by City
I built a color-coded heatmap showing guest ratings for Accuracy, Cleanliness, Communication, Location, and Value in every city. Users can switch between overall and detailed ratings.

Mexico City and Rio de Janeiro are the best rated. Hong Kong and Istanbul are the lowest. Across cities, Cleanliness and Value for money get the lowest scores.

5. Review Frequency
I checked how often the same person writes reviews. Most guests (86.5%) wrote only one review, and 98.8% wrote three or fewer.

One reviewer had 283 reviews, which looks unusual. It could be a data error or a very active traveler, so I flagged it.

6. Seasonality of Reviews
I looked at which months get the most reviews in Mexico City, New York, Paris, Rome, and Sydney.

Paris and Rome get the most reviews from April to August (European summer). New York gets a boost in November and December (holiday season).

7. Host Trust
I checked how many hosts have a verified identity and a profile picture. About 66.9% of hosts are fully verified, and only a tiny share (0.1%–0.3%) have no profile picture, so most hosts show at least one trust signal.
